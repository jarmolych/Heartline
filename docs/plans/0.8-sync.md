## Part 2: sync (branch `feat/sync-main-line`)
### What was wrong
1. The Heart's pulses only ever followed low-band onsets (kick, bass), and which of those was
   decided by a timer: the first one after an interval had passed. A riff was answered on
   whichever of its notes happened to come up, which is "only parts of the main line".
2. When the low band went quiet the beat grid filled in, and in some sections the director did
   not arm the Heart's pulses at all, leaving only enemies and darts: "background parts".
3. The player's library is piano and pads (C418, Kumi Tanioka). There the low band is the
   left-hand accompaniment and the tune is higher up, so the Heart followed the accompaniment.
   The analysis has no confidence in a beat grid on these tracks (0.00 on "Ballad of the
   Cats"), so grid-filled pulses were not on anything.
4. Ear candy was picked from "chimes": two-tone hits and pitched notes in a rarely used pitch
   band. In piano music that is any strong chord, so it fell on main beats.

### What was done
- **The main line** (`level::main_line`): every strong note in any register (low and mid
  onsets, pitched hits), with hits that land together counted as one louder note. Hi-hats are
  left out; they still drive darts and shards.
- **Songs with a steady beat** (`beat_conf >= LINE_GRID_CONF`): each section's rhythm is
  learned as the sixteenths of the bar where the line lands. The strongest places, kept far
  enough apart to be read, get a full pulse every time a note falls there. Up to two more
  places get an **echo**: three shootable notes. A one-off accent well above the usual
  strength is answered too if there is room. So a riff is answered the same way in every bar
  (`level::tests::the_heart_answers_the_same_notes_of_a_riff_every_time`).
- **Free-time music:** no bars. The Heart answers every note above a strength line drawn so
  that the count fits the section's pace, and nothing fills the gaps: it waits when the music
  waits.
- A note of the line is always answered (a small pulse if the bullet budget is spent); only
  filler beats are dropped. The Heart's pulses are armed in every section.
- `BULLETS_PER_SEC` 10 -> 8 to pay for the extra pulses and echoes.
- **Ear candy** (`analysis::find_candy`): every hit gets a fingerprint (what it added to the
  spectrum, over 24 bands, compared allowing a shift in pitch). A clearly audible hit whose
  third most similar other hit is far less alike than is usual for that song is a sound the
  song made once or twice: that is the candy, at the time it happens
  (`a_sound_the_song_makes_once_is_ear_candy`). Runs of notes still count, if the song has few.
  At most one per 40 seconds.

### How the player can help (their question)
While playing, four keys write notes to `heartline-notes.txt` next to the game:
- **T**: tap along with what the attacks should be following, for 20 to 30 seconds.
- **G** / **B**: the sync is good / off right here.
- **C**: ear candy is sounding right now.
The pause screen lists them. `heartline.exe --notes` (or `--notes-from FILE`) reads the notes
back: for the taps it scores every stream (pulses, echoes, the line's notes, low / mid / high
onsets, chimes, beats) by how many taps it meets and how much of it was tapped; for C marks it
says whether the level had candy there; for G / B it prints what the level was doing.
LULLABY (no-fail) is the easy tier to do this on.

### Not verified
Nobody has listened. The riff test and the one-off-sound test are synthetic. The fingerprint is
coarse (24 bands), so "rare" may still pick things a listener would not call candy. Balance
moved a little (plain bot, 8 Straftat songs, hits per minute LULLABY to FLATLINE, before the
last trim of the bullet budget: 1.6 / 2.2 / 4.4 / 4.8 / 5.3 / 6.3 / 6.5, against 1.6 / 1.6 /
3.8 / 3.5 / 4.3 / 5.1 / 6.3).
